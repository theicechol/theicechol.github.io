## A Thai nickname is a name of its own

In Thailand, parents commonly give their children a nickname in addition to a formal given name and family name. In Thai, a nickname is called **ชื่อเล่น** (*chue len*), literally a “play name.” It is often chosen at birth or early in childhood, rather than emerging later among friends.

What makes this tradition distinctive is how central the nickname can be. Family, friends, classmates, and colleagues may all use it as someone's everyday name. A formal name appears on official documents and in more formal introductions, while a nickname can follow a person from childhood into their professional life. The word “nickname” can make it sound optional or temporary; in Thailand, it is often simply the name people know you by.

Unlike a shortened form of a given name, a Thai nickname does not have to resemble the formal name at all. There is no need for “Ice” to be derived from “Cholpisit.” The two names can have entirely separate origins.

## Why Ice?

Thai nicknames can come from animals, foods, colors, objects, family associations, or words that parents happen to like. Many are short, though longer names are also used. English words and names are part of this naming landscape too, so a nickname such as Ice fits naturally into the tradition—even if it invites more questions when I introduce myself outside Thailand.

Families choose names for many different reasons, and there is no single rule that explains every Thai nickname. For me, the simplest introduction is still: “You can call me Ice.”
